<Chapter n="01 · Pitch" title="Mini golf," light="but it's RC racing">
  <p>The studio brief was one hole of a mini-golf course for the Whaaat!? Festival, built around a weird core interaction. Mine: you don't putt the ball, you drive it. The ball rides on top of an RC truck, and you get it through the course without letting it fall off.</p>
  <p>I prototyped three directions before committing: a golf club rigged with a gyroscope and accelerometer that steered the car by swing, a balance course where the ball rides the car, and a bumper-car version that was basically Rocket League. My testing notes were blunt about the result: the most fun part of every version was ramming cars into things.</p>

<Chapter n="02 · Prototype" title="Cardboard" light="first">
  <p>I wrote a spec with three obstacles (a ramp, a second ramp with a sand trap, and a tilt section that takes precision) and estimated the lumber at 91 square feet: three 4×8 sheets. Before buying any of it, I built the whole thing in interlocking cardboard so I could drive it, crash it and change it.</p>
  <Row cols="1fr 1fr 1fr">

<Chapter n="03 · Build" title="Get your kicks" light="on Hole 66">
  <p>The theme came from the road: Route 66. The final hole has turf fairways, sand traps, rough-cut log borders, a highway stretch painted "HIT THE GAS", and a finish where you slam into the barrier to launch the ball into a cup. The trucks wear custom 3D-printed bodies.</p>

<Chapter n="04 · Festival" title="Not how" light="I intended">
  <p>The rules sign told players: left stick forward and back, right stick turns. Work over the barriers, take it easy on rough terrain, count a stroke every time the ball falls off, then rev it up on the highway and slam into the barrier to finish. The kids at the festival had other ideas.</p>
