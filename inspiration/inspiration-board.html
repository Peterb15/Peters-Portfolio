Three directions for the new portfolio

The site is for themed entertainment studios, animation studios and freelance clients, and we're starting from a blank slate. Each direction below has a rough look, the references behind it, and what it means for Echo Was Here. Pick one, or mix parts of them.

Rules for any direction

Show the work within 5 secondsStudio recruiters skim. A reel clip or the best image should play or show above the fold, before any concept intro.
Concept → build → audienceThemed entertainment studios hire people who can take an idea through fabrication and see it in front of guests. Every case study follows that arc.
Breakdowns, not only beauty shotsAnimation studios want to see rigs, shader graphs and blocking-vs-final comparisons. Your process material is a selling point.
Resume and contact always one click awayFreelance clients need to reach you without searching. Keep a persistent contact link and a fast path past any interactive layer.

The site reads like a production package: show-set elevations, spec sheets, title blocks and work-light amber on blueprint navy. Each project is a "sheet" with real dimensions, materials and crew size. The polish comes from precision instead of decoration.

Why it fits
  • Speaks the language of themed entertainment, where ride elevations and show-set drawings are everyday documents.
  • Puts your fabrication work (battle sets, laser-cut enclosures, the table Echo) on equal footing with screen work.
  • Ages well. It won't look dated in two years.
References
  • Thinkwell Group how a themed entertainment studio presents projects
  • Meow Wolf credits how experience designers credit their roles
  • Imagineering show-set elevations and concept boards the visual vocabulary to borrow
Risk

It can feel cold. It needs one warm, human element, like a photo of you in the workshop or Echo's color breaking the blueprint.

What it does for Echo

An unfinished animation fits naturally here. Unshaded backgrounds become "Rev B" sheets beside finished shots.

Punk zine and gig-flyer energy taken straight from Echo's world: halftone, stickers, tape, collectible cards scattered over the layout, and loud flat color that matches your cel-shading. The site feels like something you'd pick up at a merch table.

Why it fits
  • The most memorable of the three. Recruiters will remember "the pink one with the stickers."
  • Shows you can build a visual identity, which is the core skill for character IP and collectibles.
  • Uses the trading cards and blind-box idea in the site's own interactions, such as flipping a card to open a project.
References
  • Superplastic vinyl toys and animated characters as one IP
  • Gorillaz a virtual band with a world around it
  • Jet Set Radio and Hi-Fi Rush cel-shading, roller skates, beat-synced motion
  • Spider-Verse halftone, comic layout and mixed frame rates
Risk

One project's look takes over the whole site, and Shattered Lands' storm-and-magic fantasy doesn't fit it. This could be a problem for more conservative studios.

What it does for Echo

Echo becomes the face of the whole site. That's great exposure for the IP, but the case study has to hold up to it.

The portfolio is a world you explore. Projects are places on a hand-drawn chart, the about page is a character sheet, and the writing lives in a library. This draws on your strongest instinct as a DM and worldbuilder, and is the approach most likely to make people say "who made this?"

Why it fits
  • Themed entertainment is about placemaking. A site that is a place makes that point before anyone reads a word.
  • Brings Shattered Lands, the writing and the tabletop work together naturally.
  • Has room to grow. New projects become new islands.
References
  • Bruno Simon the well-known drivable 3D portfolio
  • Mireia Ortega on Awwwards sound, gesture and WebGL as navigation
  • One Piece and Pangros maps your own world as the source material
Risk

A recruiter with 90 seconds may not want to explore. It needs a plain list view always visible, and it's the most build time of the three.

What it does for Echo

Echo becomes one place among several. That's balanced, but it puts less spotlight on your flagship.

The headline project

Echo Was Here as the flagship

Echo is still the right lead. It's your most recent work, it covers the widest range of skills (directing, rigging, shading, pipeline, hardware, product design), and an original character with physical merchandise is exactly what themed entertainment and animation studios look for.

An unfinished film is fine if we frame it honestly. Present it as an IP and pipeline case study, not as a finished short. The table Echo and the card set are the physical proof. The unshaded backgrounds become a "where the pipeline stands" breakdown, which animation leads like to see anyway.

One concrete ask: fully finish 10–20 seconds, your best moment from the song. That clip becomes the hero video at the top of the case study and probably the top of the whole site.
